H56 executive summary

With binary unit dots the official metric reduces exactly to DTI = T / (0.2N + 0.8G), so every dot costs the same 0.2 in the denominator no matter what it earns. The corpus that came before this file plateaued between 0.15 and 0.28 because of that arithmetic, not because of its geology: selecting the top-N pixels of an evidence field piles dots a few pixels deep on the strongest feature, where the max in the numerator saturates and the cost does not. H56 emits instead variable-density blue noise at exactly the metric's own 300 m support, using the only evidence family that measured as informative about faults the given catalogue does not contain (USGS 3DEP 1 m LiDAR scarp descriptors: enrichment 1.21–1.42, against 1.02–1.09 for every magnetic and radiometric channel), corroborated multiplicatively by 1,466 declustered USGS ComCat epicentre lineaments. Why “0.2778” reportedly worked

H33-B2 inherited a 40,199-dot owner-reported 0.2708 base and removed 2,545 dots within 200 m of the supplied catalogue. Under the 300 m distance-weighted metric, deleting low-credit dots lowers false-positive cost while preserving hidden-truth maxima. It was efficient pruning, not a new geological detector.
