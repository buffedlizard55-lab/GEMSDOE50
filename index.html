H57 executive summary

With binary unit dots the official metric reduces exactly to DTI = T / (0.2N + 0.8G), so a dot costs 0.2 whatever it earns and placement quality is everything. H57 is a greedy dot emitter at the metric's own 300 m support (3 px minimum Chebyshev spacing) over a belief field built from the two channel families that survive an elevation-stratified control: LiDAR scarp descriptors (step_max 2.73, lapneg_max 2.62, ex_mean 2.61, downface_max 2.53, ex_max 2.51, relief 2.41 pooled lift, 5/5 strata) and topographic steps (slope_max 2.61, hs_lineament 2.49, curv_prof_absmax 2.35, relief_local 2.27, slope_std 2.15, steep_frac 2.11, 5/5). The stratifier exists because the earlier screen's top channels were elevation itself (dem_mean 3.665, det_elev 3.657): F1's truth is USGS SGMC faults, which live in the mountains, so an unstratified screen was selecting terrain. The whole geodetic-strain family (0.28–0.38), every magnetic and radiometric channel (0.42–0.98) and every residualised 9×9 row are rejected by the same control.

H56 executive summary

With binary unit dots the official metric reduces exactly to DTI = T / (0.2N + 0.8G), so every dot costs the same 0.2 in the denominator no matter what it earns. The corpus that came before this file plateaued between 0.15 and 0.28 because of that arithmetic, not because of its geology: selecting the top-N pixels of an evidence field piles dots a few pixels deep on the strongest feature, where the max in the numerator saturates and the cost does not. H56 emits instead variable-density blue noise at exactly the metric's own 300 m support, using the only evidence family that measured as informative about faults the given catalogue does not contain (USGS 3DEP 1 m LiDAR scarp descriptors: enrichment 1.21–1.42, against 1.02–1.09 for every magnetic and radiometric channel), corroborated multiplicatively by 1,466 declustered USGS ComCat epicentre lineaments.
