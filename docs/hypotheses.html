Ranked hypotheses for finding faults the catalogue misses

Each item names the layers used, the physical signature, why it catches a fault missing from USGS/INGENIOUS rather than one already in it, and how it differs from what this organisation had already built.

H1 · Lidar scarp-ridge fusion, placed by independent evidence shipped

(a) layers: USGS 3DEP 1 m DEM scarp descriptors (step_max, lapneg_max, coh100) + GeoDAWN airborne radiometrics (K/Th/U/TC). (b) signature: an oriented ridge filter over the scarp-descriptor composite — a fault scarp is a linear step with a consistent facing. (c) why new: the 3DEP DEM is elevation data, not a fault inventory; it is not “captured by USGS/INGENIOUS” and it resolves scarps that the 1:250,000 compilations generalised away or never saw. (d) difference: the family's earlier ridge work used the context layers as a ranking of a smoothed seismic field; here the ridge evidence is the placement itself and is fused with the seismicity corridors.

H2 · Seismicity lineation corridors, snapped to the ridge shipped

(a) the official USGS ComCat catalogue (222,939 events; public domain) + the lidar ridge. (b) inertia-tensor anisotropy of each well-sampled event neighbourhood (λ₁/λ₂ ≥ 3, minor-axis σ ≤ 600 m), which is the 2-D reduction of the Ouillon–Sornette clustered/background separation. (c) earthquakes do not require a mapped surface trace, so a lineation can sit on a blind or poorly-mapped structure; the 2020 Monte Cristo rupture is the demonstration. (d) no earlier candidate in this organisation used the point pattern's *anisotropy* as the emission geometry, and none snapped it to the DEM ridge.

H3 · Catalogue-correction corridor (100–400 m) shipped, documented not measured

(a) the provided catalogue raster + the lidar/radiometric ridge evidence. (b) positional correction: expert/lidar re-mapping moves a trace by up to 400 m, and staff confirm such pixels count as new truth. (c) the truth is defined as “not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS”, and a corrected trace is exactly a pixel that was wrong before. (d) earlier candidates treated the catalogue's neighbourhood as forbidden; this one bets on it, but only on cells where the topography independently shows the scarp.

H4 · Drainage-offset / channel-deflection mapping not implemented

(a) 3DEP 1 m DEM + NHD flowlines (both free, official). (b) strike-slip faults offset channels; a systematic lateral deflection of successive channels along a line is a fault signature independent of scarp freshness. (c) it targets strike-slip faults in alluvium, which are the most likely to be missing from a Quaternary-scarp-focused compilation. (d) nothing in this organisation has modelled channel deflection. Cost: high (flowline ingestion, offset statistics). Not started because it could not be validated inside this session's compute budget.

H5 · InSAR / GNSS strain-rate lineaments data blocked

(a) would need a free, official InSAR velocity product (e.g. the ARIA/COMET or the USGS InSAR-based products) or the Nevada GNSS velocity field. (b) surface strain-rate gradients localise on active structures. (c) creep and interseismic strain mark faults that have no Quaternary scarp. (d) no strain-rate layer has been used in this organisation. Blocked: no free product with the right coverage was confirmed obtainable in-session; the competition's external-data rule would need re-checking too.

Where the next improvement should come from

  1. Turning H3 from a documented bet into a measured one with an independent inventory that has both a coarse and a fine mapping (the INGENIOUS QFaults map-scale attribute is the cheapest available version).
  2. H4, because channel offsets are a genuinely different physical channel from scarps and seismicity.
  3. Re-running the ledger inversion with the 16 known scores under a coarse (≤ 8 region) parameterisation, which is identifiable, rather than the 7,800-block version that failed in-sample.
