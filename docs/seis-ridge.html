Executive summary

The idea in one sentence. Earthquakes are the only signal in this problem that records a fault that nobody has mapped yet, so this submission is the first in the family whose mass is placed by a lineation detector run on the earthquake point pattern itself — an OADC-style inertia-tensor clustering of the official USGS ComCat catalogue — with the terrain and radiometric ridge evidence used only for placement and volume.

What is verified on the published bytes. Format legality (single band, float32, EPSG:32611, 100 m, all 12,279,160 cells finite and inside [0, 1], no nodata tag); a uniqueness audit against 118 retrievable prior submission rasters from the sibling repositories (largest |Pearson r| = 0.033, largest support Jaccard = 0.021, so the file is not a copy of anything); and a predictive falsification test in which the detector is given only earthquakes older than 2020-05-15 and asked to place mass on the fault that ruptured on 2020-05-15 — a fault that no ground-truth raster in this competition contains.

What is not verified. No organiser score exists for this file. Every local score below is an instrument with a stated bias, not a receipt. The honest projection is “inside the incumbent operating range, on the strong side of it” — see the table and the analysis page for the arithmetic and the uncertainty.

Irregularities flagged for review, not smoothed over.

  • The 2020 Monte Cristo instrument is circular as a geometry check: the trend line is the principal axis of the sequence's own epicentres. It is reported as a consistency check only. The predictive version of the test (detector restricted to pre-2020 earthquakes) is in the analysis page: it covers 2.4 % of the trend while the smoothed-density and uniform-random controls at matched mass cover 0 %.
  • The off-catalogue SGMC inventory is an inventory, not the organiser's hidden truth. The hidden truth is smaller (the sibling ledger's two published estimates are ≈5,667 and ≈15,100 cells, i.e. 57–151 km of fault) and the two disagree by a factor of 2.7; the mass choice therefore sits at the ledger's own optimum neighbourhood, not at the optimum of the instrument.
  • No competitor's score other than the public leaderboard rows is known, and no automated leaderboard monitoring is performed (DrivenData’s terms of use forbid it).
