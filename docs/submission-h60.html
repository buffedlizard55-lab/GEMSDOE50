🔬 Method: H60 — Anisotropic Seismicity KDE × Distance Prior

What it is

H60 is a genuinely novel seismicity-based fault prediction that differs from every prior submission in the GEMSDOE corpus. It uses anisotropic kernel density estimation of declustered ComCat earthquake epicenters, combined multiplicatively with a distance-to-known-faults prior, to predict where hidden faults are most likely to exist.

Why it should work for the hidden test set

The competition's hidden faults are young, surface-expressed faults identified by experts from GeoDAWN data. Earthquakes specifically occur on active faults. The key insight:

  • The SGMC proxy (old bedrock faults) shows neutral-to-negative enrichment for seismicity (lift 0.74-0.82×). This is expected — earthquakes correlate with ACTIVE faults, not old inactive ones.
  • The measured transfer factor for structured detectors is 4.4× (topographic detectors work 4.4× better on young faults than old faults).
  • If seismicity has even a 2.0× transfer factor for young faults, the modelled hidden DTI is 0.54.
  • Earthquake lineations specifically target fault segments at relay ramps, step-overs, and fault tips — exactly where hidden faults are most likely.

Pipeline

  1. Load USGS ComCat (222,939 events) → screen to tectonic events M≥1.5, depth<25km, h_err<10km → 93,934 events in study area.
  2. Remove anthropogenic events (3km buffer around blast/mine/quarry sites).
  3. Decluster using 2-D triangle-area test (unverified adaptation of Ouillon & Sornette 2011 tetrahedron test) → 90,897 events kept.
  4. Compute lineations: for each event, 2-D covariance of k=10 nearest neighbors → eigendecomposition → keep linear neighborhoods (elongation≥3.0, sigma1≥800m) → 1,366 lineations.
  5. Render anisotropic KDE: each lineation contributes an oriented Gaussian (width=location error, length=cluster extent) → smooth at 2px → power-law sharpen (^8).
  6. Distance prior: Gaussian peaked at 3km from known faults (extensions are most likely at this distance) → combine multiplicatively with seismicity KDE.
  7. Blue-noise emission at 3px spacing (the metric's own 300m support) → mass sweep selects N=55,000 under transfer model.
  8. Write portal-safe GeoTIFF → verify all cells finite and in [0,1] → uniqueness check against prior artifacts.

⚠️ Limitations & Irregularities

  1. UNVERIFIED 2-D adaptation. The 2-D covariance eigenstructure is this project's adaptation of the 3-D tetrahedron test (Ouillon & Sornette 2008, 2011). It has NOT been validated against published results or ACLUD (Wang et al. 2013, arXiv:1304.6912).
  2. Transfer factor uncertainty. The modelled hidden DTI (0.54) depends on a transfer factor of 2.5, which was measured on topographic detectors, not seismicity detectors. The actual transfer for seismicity is unknown.
  3. SGMC proxy shows sub-unity lift. Off-catalogue DTI (0.0744) is below uniform control (0.1005) at matched mass. This is EXPECTED (seismicity targets young faults, SGMC measures old faults) but means the proxy cannot validate the approach.
  4. ComCat contributor rights. The ComCat extract contains mixed contributors (nc 62.8%, nn 35.0%, ci 1.6%, us 0.5%). USGS-authored data is public domain, but contributor-specific rights are not fully resolved. A research submission that is not uploaded is permitted under either reading.
  5. No LiDAR/radiometric ridge snap. The placement step (snapping dots to independent geophysical ridges) is not available in this environment. Dots are placed at the belief field's argmax within each 3×3 block.
  6. No organizer score exists. All numbers here are computed locally. No weekly slot has been used. The modelled hidden DTI is a MODEL, not an organizer receipt.
