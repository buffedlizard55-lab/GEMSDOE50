## 2. Why the seismic method fails here (diagnostics)

1. **Conditional on scarp strength, corridors are depleted, not enriched.**
   Within each H57-belief decile, the metric credit field `K` is lower inside the
   corridors than outside in **10 of 10 deciles** (median ratio 0.065; top decile
   0.278). Epicentral, up-dip-left and up-dip-right dots all sit below the uniform
   base rate (mean K 0.0100 / 0.0087 / 0.0098 vs 0.0352).
2. **Not an artifact of over-screening** (post-hoc sensitivity, exploratory):
   dropping the triangle screen (171 lineations), dropping declustering too
   (228), or relaxing the lineation rules (1,034 lineations, 6,643 dots) lifts
   corridor dots only slightly above the base rate (mean K 0.053–0.061 vs 0.042,
   thanks to the ridge snap), and the stratified enrichment stays **below 1** in
   every variant (0.85–0.97).
3. **Physical reading.** The region's well-located micro-seismicity sits at a
   median 5.8 km depth on structures that are either already in the supplied
   catalogue (excluded by the 300 m buffer) or in volcanic/swarm settings with
   little mapped surface faulting. The up-dip projection (`Δ = z/tan 60° ≈ 3.3 km`)
   multiplies the location uncertainty (corridor half-width hits the 2 km cap),
   so the corridor adds area without adding precision. This is the third
   independent failure of seismicity geometry in this repository (H55 relocated
   catalogue, H58-S1 epicentral corridors, H61-S up-dip corridors).
4. **Design flaw found in my own frozen constants** (documented, not hidden):
   with 12 nearest neighbours, a dense sequence spans too little length to pass
   the "deconvolved half-length ≥ 1 km" rule, so 2,008 of 2,307 neighbourhoods
   failed on length (`tests/test_h61.py::test_frozen_length_rule_rejects_dense_lines`).
   The lenient sensitivity variant removes exactly this restriction and the
   conclusion does not change.

## 3. Why wider spacing does not help (H61-M falsified)

The repository believed 3 px spacing was competition-free; it is not (§4). But
removing the competition by spacing dots 4–5 px apart *lowers* the score at fixed
mass: the extra dots must go to lower-belief cells, and that dilution costs more
than the shared credit it saves. At 80,000 dots on the H57 field, H57's own 3 px
emission is the best of the three spacings in 3 of 4 folds.

## 4. Metric algebra — correction to earlier documents

The repository's `docs/research/h56-diagnosis.md` (eq. 1) and the H56/H57 bands
state that with binary dots ≥ 3 px apart the official score reduces *exactly* to
`DTI = T / (0.2 N + 0.8 G)`. That is false. From the official definition
`DTI = TP / (TP + 0.2 FP + 0.8 FN)` with `FN = G − TP` and the distance-weighted
`FP = Σ_dots (1 − K(dot))`:

```
DTI = T / (0.2 N + 0.8 G + 0.2 (T − M)),   M = Σ_dots K(dot) = N − FP
1/DTI = 0.2 + (0.2 FP + 0.8 G) / T
```

Synthetic check with the repository's own instrument (160-px fault + 200 far
dots; `tests/test_h61.py`): on-line dots at 3 px score **0.6462**, the claimed
"exact" formula gives 0.6972; dots 1 px off the line 0.4844 vs 0.5147; on-line
dots at 6 px 0.4348 vs 0.4614. The collapse is exact only when `T = M`.

Second false claim: "spacing ≥ 3 px ⇒ no two dots compete." Two dots compete for
a truth pixel whenever both are within 3 px of it, i.e. at spacing < 6 px.
Per-dot credit on a straight fault: 1.000 / 1.667 / 2.333 / 2.667 / 3.000 / 3.000
for spacing 1…6 px (tested).

**Consequence for the required-credit tables.** For a target score `s`:
`T > s (0.2 FP + 0.8 G) / (1 − 0.2 s)`. Writing `M = T/c` (c ≈ 2.33 for on-line
dots at 3 px), `T > s (0.2 N + 0.8 G) / (1 − 0.2 s (1 − 1/c))`. At `s = 0.3774`
the old tables understated the credit needed by ≈ 4.5 %.

No frozen holdout number changes (those were always computed with the
official-equivalent instrument); the analytic tables and design rationale do.

## 5. GEMSDOE32 H33-B2 = 0.2778 — mechanism, with the corrected algebra

Owner-reported scores, no organizer receipt in any repository (flag F4 below).
The B2 bytes are the d2.8 artifact (44,090 dots, owner-reported 0.2600) with the
6,436 dots lying ≤ 2 px from the supplied catalogue removed (37,654 dots;
zeros SHA-256 `c55bafc47005…`, GEMSDOE32 commit `b983924b`). A conflicting
GEMSDOE32 text (40,199 − 2,545) remains unresolved.

If the removed dots earned ~zero hidden credit (the earlier study measured the
proxy credit nearly unchanged, 4,836 → 4,809), the two scores pin the hidden
quantities. With `1/s = 0.2 + (0.2 FP + 0.8 G)/T`:

* d2.8: `(0.2 FP₁ + 0.8 G)/T = 1/0.2600 − 0.2 = 3.646`
* B2:   `(0.2 (FP₁ − 6,436) + 0.8 G)/T = 1/0.2778 − 0.2 = 3.400`

Subtracting: `0.2 · 6,436 / T = 0.2465` ⇒ **T ≈ 5,223 credit** (credit/dot ≈ 0.139), and
with `M ≈ T/2.33` ⇒ **G ≈ 13,343 hidden truth cells** (model-based; the earlier
12,226 / 14,089 estimates used the wrong collapse). So B2's +0.0178 is pure
**dot economy**: 6,436 dots × 0.2 false-positive weight removed, no new
detection. The mechanism is real and portable (prune dots that cannot earn), but
it is not a better detector — which is why it sits at only 0.0878 on the frozen
gate.

**Can we beat it?** Under the same model (G ≈ 13,343, FP = N − T/2.33), at 80,000
dots H57 needs T > 7,653 hidden credit (credit/dot > 0.0957) to beat 0.2778; to
beat **0.3774** it needs T > 10,521 (credit/dot > 0.1315), and at 40,000 dots
T > 7,365 (credit/dot > 0.1841, i.e. +33 % over B2's ≈ 0.139). Computed, not
hand-derived: `1/(1 − 0.2·0.3774·(1 − 1/2.33)) = 1.045`, the 4.5 % understatement
in the old tables. H57's earlier calibration
(`h57-verdict-20261007.md`) gives an expected ≈ 0.23 with P(> 0.2778) ≈ 29 % and
P(> 0.3774) ≈ 4 %. Nothing measured in H61 changes that.
