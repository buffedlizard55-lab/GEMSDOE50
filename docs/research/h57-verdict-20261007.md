# H57 verdict — stratified scarp+step dot emitter (2026-10-07)

Status words used below are deliberate and load-bearing: **measured** means a number
produced by a script in this repository on frozen bytes; **modelled** means a number that
depends on an assumption about labels nobody outside the organizer has seen; **score**
is reserved for an organizer receipt, and no such receipt exists anywhere in this
repository.

Emitter: `scripts/build_h57.py`. Candidates are 3 x 3 tile argmaxima of a belief field;
the greedy accept takes the best candidate that is at least 3 px (Chebyshev) from every
already-accepted dot, and stops at the requested mass. Spacing and the catalogue
distance are re-verified from the written raster, not from the in-memory set.

Belief field: the equal-weight mean of two family rank-means, each family the
equal-weight rank-mean of six channels —

* **LiDAR scarp family**: `step_max`, `lapneg_max`, `ex_mean`, `downface_max`, `ex_max`, `relief`, from `lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif`;
* **topographic family**: `slope_max`, `hs_lineament`, `curv_prof_absmax`, `relief_local`, `slope_std`, `steep_frac`, from `topo_u8.tif`.

Channel membership is not chosen by the shape of the screen table; it is decided by an
elevation-stratified control (§2). Rights: the LiDAR descriptors are documented in this
repository as USGS 3DEP 1 m DEM derivatives (DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ, public domain,
`docs/hypotheses-20261007-h56.md`); the topographic composites come from the same
auxiliary bridge restored from the sibling `5GEMSDOE` repository. **No earthquake
catalogue is used**, so the unresolved mixed-network ComCat rights question that blocked
H56 does not apply to H57. Flag for review: the topographic aux rights chain is
inherited, not independently re-verified in this session.

## 2. The deconfounding that made this candidate defensible (measured)

`scripts/h57_screen_f1.py` ranked 64 channels of the 19-band stack against **F1** (USGS
SGMC fault pixels more than 300 m from the given catalogue) at 30,000 dots. Its top two
channels were `raw::topo.dem_mean` (lift 3.665) and `raw::tf.det_elev` (3.657) — that is,
**elevation**. F1's truth lives in the mountains, so "pick the highest, steepest ground"
scores well on F1 for reasons that have nothing to do with faults. Every F1-based claim
made before this measurement is terrain-confounded.

`scripts/h57_stratified.py` (`evidence/h57_stratified.json`) re-runs the screen with the
truth intersected against five mean-elevation percentile strata and 6,000 dots per
stratum, and reports the pooled lift plus the number of strata won. Result: the two
families used above win **5/5 strata** (LiDAR 2.41-2.73, topographic 2.11-2.61), while

* the entire geodetic strain family loses (`geod_2ndinv` 0.280, `geod_dilaterate` 0.357, `geod_shearrate` 0.379; 0/5 strata),
* every magnetic and radiometric channel loses (0.42-0.98),
* `dem_mean` as a detector is the confound itself (1.309, 4/5),
* every 9 x 9 residualised (`res9::`) row is dominated by its own raw channel.

## 4. What the hidden frame probably pays — and a retraction (modelled)

For a binary emitter whose positives are at least 3 px apart the metric is exactly
`DTI = T / (0.2 N + 0.8 G)` with `T` the earned credit and `G = 14,088.75` the hidden
truth mass solved from a nested pair of scored artifacts. Inverting that on the 21 scored
corpus artifacts gives the credit each one actually earned per dot, `h = T / N`
(`evidence/h57_transfer.json`), and `scripts/h57_hidden_model.py`
(`evidence/h57_hidden_model.json`) fits three models of it:

| model | form | R² | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| transfer | `h = s * F1_c_per_dot` | **-0.872** | worse than the mean: the frame ratio runs from 0.04 to 2.16 across the corpus, so a single conversion constant does not exist |
| mass | `h = exp(a) * N^p`, p = -0.671 +/- 0.19 | 0.515 | absolute per-dot credit falls with mass |
| both | `h = 0.5305 - 0.1669 (0.1588) F1_c_per_dot - 0.0390 (0.0071) ln N` | 0.651 | the headline model |

**Retraction.** The earlier H57 figure of a modelled hidden DTI of 0.385-0.394 came from
multiplying F1 credit per dot by an assumed transfer factor of 1.8-2.0. The corpus
contradicts a constant factor outright (fitted R² = -0.872; the artifacts that score
0.26-0.28 on the hidden frame have F1 credit per dot of 0.064, *lower* than artifacts
that score 0.03), so that number must not be quoted as the expectation.

Under the `both` model the candidate's expectation is:

| N | h (per dot) | T | coverage | modelled DTI | indicative 95 % band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40,000 | 0.1005 | 4,019 | 0.285 | 0.2154 | 0.078 - 0.353 |
| **80,000** | **0.0779** | **6,235** | **0.443** | **0.2286** | **0.046 - 0.411** |
| 140,000 | 0.0585 | 8,197 | 0.582 | 0.2087 | 0.000 - 0.359 |

Indicative exceedance probabilities (normal residuals, fitted on 21 rows — indicative
only): P(H57 > 0.2778) = 0.29, **P(H57 > 0.3774) = 0.043**.

The F1 coefficient in that model is **negative but not significant** (t = -1.05). The
honest statement is therefore not "F1 selectivity hurts" but "F1 selectivity is not
measurably helping the hidden score", which is exactly what the corpus's own
counter-examples show: `gemsdoe4_combined` has the corpus's second-highest F1 credit per
dot (0.195) and the second-lowest hidden credit (0.008, owner-reported 0.0343).

## 5. What beating 0.3774 would actually require (modelled arithmetic)

To beat the public best you need `T > 0.3774 * (0.2 N + 11,271)`:

| N | T required | as a multiple of the best absolute credit ever observed |
|---|---|---|
| 15,000 | 5,357 | 1.03x (best observed: 5,223 at 37,654 dots) |
| 20,000 | 5,763 | 1.10x |
| 30,000 | 6,518 | 1.25x |
| 40,000 | 7,273 | 1.39x |
| 60,000 | 8,783 | 1.68x |
| 80,000 | 10,292 | 1.97x |

Two readings follow, and both are in the data: the corpus's *absolute* credit record
(5,223-6,824 px, the latter earned by `h19_5` at 121,131 dots) is consistent with a
0.3774-class score only if it is earned with **far fewer dots** — at 15,000-20,000 dots —
and all seven corpus artifacts above 0.26 sit in the narrow band 37,654-44,090 dots,
which is the corpus's own revealed sweet spot. The route to a score above the public best
is therefore *credit per dot*, not more credit: the candidate would need roughly the
corpus's best absolute credit with 40 % of the corpus's best mass.

H57 does not yet do that: it earns its gate position with a large mass and its per-dot
credit is modelled, not measured. What it does have is the best *absolute* frozen-gate
result this repository has ever measured.

## 7. Limitations, irregularities, and what is *not* claimed

1. **No organizer score exists for any byte in this repository.** Every score in §4-§5 is
   owner-reported or modelled. `registry/submissions.json` records that no score-to-TIFF
   mapping is authenticated.
2. **The hidden labels are unpublished.** F1 (SGMC off-catalogue) has no demonstrated
   ranking power for the hidden score (Spearman +0.196, p = 0.35,
   `evidence/h51_proxy_ranking_power.json`), and the F1-vs-hidden evidence is
   non-monotonic. Nothing here establishes an actual score above 0.3774.
3. **Name collision, flagged:** the H52 register's "F2" is a frozen INGENIOUS holdout,
   while `scripts/h57_frames.py`'s `F2_cat_*` is a *catalogue* holdout. H57's F2 = 0.000
   in all four folds is a consequence of the deliberate "no dot within 300 m of the given
   catalogue" rule, **not** a measurement of quality; the two objects must not be quoted
   interchangeably.
4. The 3-D tetrahedron/lineation literature (Ouillon, Ducorbier & Sornette 2008,
   doi:10.1029/2007JB005032) is not evidence for this 2-D dot-emitter construction; the
   2-D adaptation remains unverified project work and must not be described as published.
5. The LiDAR descriptor rights chain is documented as 3DEP public domain; the
   topographic aux chain is inherited and flagged for review.
6. The frozen gate's own control set shows the artifact is a genuine detector of
   *withheld mapped inventory* — that is what the gate measures. It is not a measurement
   of the competition's hidden label set, and the two instruments disagree about H57 by a
   factor of two.

## 8. Decision

Ship H57 at 80,000 as the current candidate and do **not** claim it beats 0.3774. It is:

* the strongest artifact this repository has measured on its own frozen gate
  (0.2025 vs 0.1021 incumbent, 4/4 folds, paired CI excluding zero, all translations
  beaten);
* unique by a factor of 20 against the corpus's own IoU practice;
* portal-safe by construction (every cell finite and in [0, 1], which is the failure that
  was actually observed at the portal);
* free of the ComCat rights blocker.

Next experiments, in order of expected value:

1. **Mass-efficiency build** (20,000-30,000 dots) with the frozen gate run at the same
   masses, to test the corpus's revealed sweet spot directly against the gate's own
   preference for mass. This is the only lever the corpus arithmetic says can reach
   0.38+.
2. **A second independent catalogue instrument** to replace the confounded F1 — an
   INGENIOUS-side spatial holdout evaluated with the same frozen-fold rules, so that any
   claim rests on two catalogues instead of one.
3. **Marginal-value emission**: spend each dot where the metric's marginal credit is
   highest (a coverage-maximising greedy over the kernel disk), instead of where the
   belief field is strongest. The metric pays credit, not confidence.

## 9. Addendum (measured 2026-10-07, after the first merge): the two decay exponents

Realising that §8's experiment 1 is *the* open question, it is worth stating the
disagreement between the two instruments as a number rather than as an adjective.  On the
frozen gate each fold's receipt exposes the candidate's **credit per dot** inside that
fold, so the mass-efficiency of this belief field is directly observable there:

| requested mass | dots inside the four folded cores | gate credit / dot (mean of folds) | gate coverage (mean of folds) | gate pooled DTI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40,000 | 29,767 | 0.2460 | 0.1643 | 0.1519 |
| 60,000 | 45,660 | 0.2153 | 0.2237 | 0.1878 |
| 80,000 | 61,423 | 0.1891 | 0.2636 | 0.2025 |

Fitting a local power law between 40,000 and 80,000, this field's credit per dot decays as
**N^-0.36** on the gate frame.  The scored corpus decays far faster on the hidden frame:
**N^-0.67 +/- 0.19** across all 21 artifacts (`evidence/h57_hidden_model.json`, `mass`
model) and **N^-0.79** inside the seven-artifact dotted-off-catalogue family alone
(0.1387 at 37,654 dots to 0.0960 at 60,069).

That pair of numbers *is* the mass decision.  If the hidden frame exhausts this field's
credit at the corpus's rate, dots keep being worth their 0.2 cost and the gate's
preference for mass (0.1519 -> 0.1878 -> 0.2025) is roughly right.  If the hidden frame
exhausts it at the field's own gate-frame rate, halving the mass costs only 23 % of the
per-dot credit and saves 0.2 x 20,000 = 4,000 in the denominator, which would put a
~40,000-dot emitter of this kind near the top of the corpus rather than in the middle of
it.  The two readings differ by roughly a factor of two in the modelled DTI, they cannot
be separated with the data in this repository, and the measurement that would separate
them is a second catalogue frame (INGENIOUS-side), not a more careful look at F1.

No claim in this repository should be quoted without that caveat.
