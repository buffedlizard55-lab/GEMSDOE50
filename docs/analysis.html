1. The metric is a budget, and the budget has a price

For a prediction p and truth g the organiser’s score is

DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw + ε), with k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0).

Three identities were re-derived and tested here (tests/test_metric.py, 21 tests; exact value vs a brute-force transcription agree to 1e-6, the gap being float32 accumulation):

  1. FNw = |G| − TPw, so with T = TPw, S the emitted mass and M the part of that mass which best-covers some truth cell, DTI = T / (0.2·(T + S − M) + 0.8·|G|).
  2. Scaling a prediction by s gives DTI(s) = sT / (0.2·s(T+S−M) + 0.8|G|), which is increasing in s — so the optimum over p ∈ [0,1] is at a vertex: a binary raster is the right representation. (This is why the family’s best files are sparse “dotted” rasters.)
  3. Adding a unit of mass whose kernel weight is k raises DTI iff k > 0.2·DTI. If the historical owner-reported 0.2778 were the applicable DTI, the illustrative price is 0.0556 credit; 0.2778 is not verified as an organizer score or current incumbent.

4. Conditional metric arithmetic (not a leaderboard target)

For any assumed target score s, truth size G, and emitted mass M, use the metric identity T = s(0.2M + 0.8G)/(1 − 0.2s) to reason about required truth credit. This is an algebraic sensitivity calculation only. The prior sibling-repository proxy scores and instrument scalings below have not been independently authenticated as organizer results, and they do not predict a current score or identify which TIFF was scored.

5. The falsification test the brief demanded, run so it cannot be circular

The 2020 Mw 6.5 Monte Cristo Range earthquake ruptured a fault that no ground-truth raster in this competition contains — the one unmapped rupture in the survey area with independent ground truth. The detector is therefore given only earthquakes older than 2020-05-15 (74,147 events, 1,207 of them within 40 km of the rupture) and asked where the mass should go. Verdict. (i) On the unmapped rupture, the corridor detector is the only field that places any mass within 300 m of the rupture — the two smoothed-density controls the brief named and the uniform-random control cover 0 % of it. (ii) On the given catalogue, the density fields beat the corridors (0.039–0.052 vs 0.027 credit per cell): the corridor detector is demonstrably not a better predictor of mapped faults, which is the honest negative half of the test. (iii) On the broad off-catalogue inventory at this very small mass, uniform random beats every structured field (0.103 vs ≤0.003) — a measured demonstration that the off-catalogue inventory instrument rewards dispersion at low mass, which is why every comparison in this repository is made at the ledger’s operating mass instead.

With the full catalogue (2020–2022 events included, therefore circular for the rupture) the shipped field covers 36 % of the same trend at 0.0022 credit per cell, versus 11 % and 0.0008 for the incumbent and 11 % and 0.0006 for the previous best dotted file. The pre-2020 result is the one that counts as evidence; the circular one is a consistency check, labelled as such everywhere.

6. Irregularities and open risks, stated rather than smoothed

  • The SGMC / QFaults compilations are USGS-family inventories and may be the organisers’ own label source; using them as an emission layer would be indistinguishable from leakage. They are used here only as a measurement instrument, and the one place where they are an input (H50-3) is explicitly not shipped.
  • The two hidden-truth-size estimates disagree by a factor of 2.7 (§3). If the true value is 5,667, this file’s mass is 19 % above its optimum and the score is ~0.03 lower than it could have been.
  • The corridor detector needs M ≥ 1.5 events at depth ≤ 20 km from 1970; the official catalogue is complete at that level only from roughly the 2000s, so detection power decreases with fault age — the opposite of the terrain evidence, which sees only cumulative displacement.
  • The 3DEP lidar descriptor layer covers 75 % of the survey; corridors outside it are emitted unsnapped. This is reported per-corridor in the build receipt.
