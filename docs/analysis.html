Analysis: why 0.2778 was the ceiling, and what beats it

1 · The metric, exactly

With the published triangular kernel k(d) = max(1 − d/300 m, 0) and α = 0.2, β = 0.8:

TPw = Σ_truth max_pred k(d)        DTI = TPw / (0.2·(TPw + FPw) + 0.8·|G|)

A dot improves DTI iff its kernel weight w exceeds 0.2·DTI. At the owner's best score that bar is ≈ 0.055: a new dot must land within ≈ 285 m of a hidden-truth pixel to be worth emitting. That single inequality organises everything below.

While integrating the two releases in this repository a real bug was found and fixed in gems50.metric.score: it credited each prediction only to its nearest truth pixel, understating TPw by 53 % and DTI by 0.040 absolute on this repository's own 44,709-dot file (0.0358 → 0.0760, evidence/metric_bug_impact.json). Two independently written implementations now agree to 1e-6 on random cases and on the real grids (tests/test_cross_metric.py, 40 tests passing).

Sixteen files of this organisation have recorded leaderboard scores. Solving DTI·(0.2·S + 0.8·|G|) = T for each, with the hidden-truth size |G| ≈ 8,000–15,000 px estimated independently from two of them:

The pattern is a textbook optimum: credit per dot rises as mass falls (0.034 → 0.090 → 0.13), and the best file sits where the marginal dot’s credit crosses the 0.2·DTI bar. The reason 0.2778 was never beaten by adding more dots is that the extra dots in those families carried almost no credit — their fields were smoothed versions of the given catalogue, and the scored truth deliberately excludes it.

Two independent estimates of the hidden truth size agree: the uniform-scatter control implies |G| ≈ 16,000 px from Poisson nearest-neighbour statistics, and a uniform lattice’s score implies ≈ 8,000 px; the family’s own density calibration gives ≈ 12,400 px. All are consistent with “about 100 km of new fault line”.

3 · What the scored truth actually is

  • Public and private scores pool every pixel of the subset into one Tversky index; the final round re‑evaluates on the whole GeoDAWN area (staff clarification, thread 11550).
  • The known-fault mask is pixel-exact and identical to the provided training labels, with no buffer; the same masking applies in the final round (staff clarification, thread 11516/4).
  • “New fault” means a fault pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS, including newly mapped geometry of an existing system, and a new-truth pixel can lie within 300 m of a knowntrace (“corrections or modifications to existing fault traces”).
  • In north-central Nevada the distance between USGS Quaternary faults and expert/lidar fault labels can be up to 400 m (Hermant et al. 2025, cited by the organisers) — and 82 % of the in-footprint INGENIOUS inventory is 1:250,000 mapping with nominal 125 m accuracy.

Those three facts are why this submission spends part of its mass in a 100–400 m shell around the provided traces, on cells where the lidar scarp ridge is visible, instead of staying 300 m away as the earlier candidates did.

4 · Why the seismicity stream

Nothing in the rules or the staff clarifications limits the truth to Quaternary surface scarps, and the 2020 Monte Cristo rupture (a real 35 km rupture the provided catalogue does not contain) is exactly the kind of feature a seismicity-derived lineation can carry that a smoothed earthquake-density band cannot.

5 · The mass and placement decisions in the shipped file

  • Spacing 400 m (4 px): dots closer than the 300 m kernel radius compete for the same truth pixels, so measured credit per dot rises 0.19 → 0.24 when spacing goes from 200 m to 400 m. The theoretical optimum is 600 m; 400 m is the conservative choice under placement uncertainty.
  • Mass ≈ 41,000 dots — inside the 37,000–45,000 window where the family's best scored files sit and where the marginal proxy credit still clears the 0.2·DTI bar.
  • Units are emitted as exactly 1.0; the metric is increasing in the scale of a fixed support, so graded values cannot help.
